# Discriminating experiments

State which question or hypotheses the experiment addresses and what observations they predict. Define manipulated variables, controls, measurement/estimand, analysis, resource bounds and stopping rules. Consider explanations from leakage, selection, implementation bugs or measurement artifacts before attributing a result to the proposed mechanism.

Exploratory work may discover a pattern without a predeclared directional prediction. Label it exploratory, then design an appropriate confirmation if a stronger claim needs it. Confirmatory plans require predictions recorded before the run. Log changes made after looking at evidence with a new revision and reason.

Choose baselines, ablations, repeats, held-out conditions and uncertainty analysis according to the question. Do not impose them indiscriminately on a proof, survey or small diagnostic. Map the scientific design to code/config and sanity-check measurements before expensive runs.

Record raw outputs, failures, negative observations and comparison with predictions. A failed program is not a refutation of the hypothesis. Preserve the exact plan/hypothesis version as a subject reference. Scientific conclusions use dependency references to the evidence actually considered.
